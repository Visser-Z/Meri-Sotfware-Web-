How consignment works

You're not
there when
it sells.

Produce is placed with a market agent, sold off the floor over days, and paid out less the agent's deductions. The only account of what happened is the agent's reports, and most businesses still type them into Excel by hand. MURR takes that job over and checks the work.

  1. 01

    Produce goes to market

    Deliveries leave for the wholesale markets on consignment. Nothing is bought, so nothing is known until the agent reports.

  2. 02

    Agents sell and report

    Daily sales, account-sale statements and payment runs arrive as PDFs and CSVs, each in its own format, often days apart.

  3. 03

    MURR does the rest

    Reads every report, updates your book, matches payments to sales, settles suppliers and shows where the money and stock stand.

01 / Sales capture

Drop in the reports. MURR keeps the book.

Drop the agents' Daily Sales Detail, as PDF or CSV and several at once. MURR reads every docket, works out what goes on the book and shows it to you before anything is saved.

  • Recognises each report by what's in it, not its file name, and refuses one loaded in the wrong place
  • Splits each delivery by the payment run it sold under, and keeps returns visible instead of folding them away
  • Appends to your existing Excel workbook with your formulas intact and your notes column untouched
  • Flags anything it can't prove, like a missing delivery note or product code, and remembers your answer next time
Sales & Payments · Review
02 / Payments & reconciliation

Every payment matched to the sales it pays for.

Drop the Payment Details and MURR matches each payment to its sales by account-sale number. That's an exact match, with no near misses.

  • Each sale is marked matched, outstanding, over or unpaid, accurate to the cent
  • A payment's nett is split across its products by sales value, so the rows always add up exactly
  • Agent deductions are compared with your own usual rate, and anything well above it is flagged with the figures
  • Payments the report doesn't break down are shown as unattributed, never quietly dropped
Sales & Payments · Reconcile
03 / Tracking

What's been paid, what's still to come, and what's on the floor.

A running position across the whole book, so you always know your exposure. You can narrow it to any month or week.

  • Paid versus still to come, with outstanding money grouped by market and agent and how long it has waited
  • Stock on hand at every market, with lines sitting too long marked red or orange
  • Payments it can't place with certainty are listed to check, and moved to the right sale in one click
  • Sales per day, with what was paid and what is still owed for each day
Tracking
04 / Insights

What sells, where, and how the agents are treating your money.

A dashboard over your saved history, for all time, a month or a single week.

  • Sales value and cartons, with what sold, what came back and the net shown separately
  • Best sellers by fruit, and totals by market and agent, each opening to show product detail
  • Statements that kept well above your usual deduction are flagged, with the numbers that raised the flag
  • Open about its limits: where the agents' exports leave a figure blank, MURR says so
Insights · By market
05 / Reports

Any two dates. The full picture.

Pick a range or a whole month. Sales are counted on the day they sold, and payments on the day they arrived.

  • Sales value, payments received, nett after the agent's cut, and how much is reconciled
  • A day-by-day chart, plus tables by product, by day and by agent
  • Cartons, returns, days to sell and average price per carton for each product
  • Export to Excel or print in one click
Reports · September 2026
06 / Procurement

What to take on next month, how much, and where to send it.

A buy plan worked out from what has actually cleared, how fast, at what price and how recently. It's calculated, not guessed, so the same history always gives the same plan.

  • Cartons to take on and what the market should return, minus what is still on the floor
  • Each product is marked high, steady or hold, with the reasons in plain English
  • Room to grow and test loads, where a product has outperformed or never been tried
  • When the money lands: how quickly each agent pays, plus an order sheet as a PDF
Procurement · November plan
07 / Assistant

Ask your book a question in plain English.

"Which market pays the most for grapes?" "What should I buy more of next month?" Answers come from your recorded history, and conversations are kept so you can pick up where you left off.

  • Every total and ranking is calculated by the same code behind the dashboard. The AI only explains the answer.
  • Read-only by design: it can never change your book or your history
  • It tells you when goods came back, so a net figure isn't mistaken for the whole story
  • Open it from any screen, including straight from the buy plan

Rules MURR
won't break.

You settle real money with real suppliers off these numbers. A figure that's quietly wrong is worse than a gap you can see, so MURR is built around a few firm rules.

01

No figure is invented

Where a report doesn't carry a number, MURR asks for it once or shows the gap. It never fills it with a default.

02

Your workbook stays yours

New rows are appended beneath your own. Formulas are kept and your notes column is never written to.

03

No settlement at a guessed rate

A supplier is never owed money worked out from a missing deal. Unsold stock never becomes a debt.

04

Every flag shows its numbers

Nothing is called fraud. A high deduction is a question with the figures attached, not an accusation.

05

The AI never does the maths

Totals are calculated by code. The assistant only explains them and can't change a single record.

06

Access is controlled

Invite-only sign-in. Staff and admins see only what they're allowed to, and history can't be wiped in one go.
